The problem: Space debris between 1mm–10cm is too small for the U.S. Space Surveillance Network to individually track, yet capable of mission-ending damage at orbital velocities. Less than 1% of debris in this danger category is currently tracked. Operators who most need this picture — university cubesat teams, early-stage startups, independent researchers — are locked out of official conjunction data. And a risk score alone isn't a decision: it doesn't say what it would cost to act on, or which threat deserves attention first.

◎  The Pipeline

Three stages. Every number is earned.

Each stage produces inspectable, deterministic output. Click any result in the app to see the exact formula and constants behind it. No model ever guesses a risk score or a delta-v figure.

Stage 01

◎

Characterize

Given a debris light curve (brightness over time as an object tumbles), estimate size, shape, and rotation state using Fourier decomposition and amplitude-based inversion. ML refines on top of those physical estimates — math is always primary.

Fourier · FFT · RandomForest

Stage 02

⊞

Map

Characterised detections combine with TLE-tracked object density into a grid-based risk-density map across 8 LEO altitude bands, weighted by recency and confidence. Updated with each detection batch. Not a Bayesian filter — stated plainly throughout.

SGP4 · CelesTrak · Recency-weighted

Stage 03

△

Prioritize & Act

Each high-risk region is costed, not just ranked. Hohmann transfer + plane-change delta-v per altitude band, weighed against risk removed: risk × severity ÷ Δv. Tiers: HIGH-PRIORITY / MONITOR / LOW. Exports CSV + JSON with delta-v on every entry.

Hohmann · Region-level only · CSV/JSON

⊕  Design Principles

Built to be used, not just viewed.

∿

Math is always deterministic

Every risk score, rotation rate, and delta-v figure is computed in code from physics constants. No model generates or guesses a number.

⊕

Every output is inspectable

Click any result to see the exact formula, constants, and intermediate values behind it. Nothing is a black box.

△

Region-level costing only

Delta-v is costed at the altitude-band level — not per object. Stage 1 gives size/shape/rotation, not a state vector. The math is honest about what the data supports.

◉

IBM Granite translates, never generates

Granite receives computed numbers and writes plain-English analyst briefs. It never invents a value. Substantive fallback text runs without any credentials.

≡  Data Transparency

Nothing overstated as live.

Light-curve training data: physics-based synthetic (Lambertian + specular reflection, Kaasalainen & Torppa 2001). Labeled # SYNTHETIC DATA throughout.  ·  Validation: real Mini-MegaTORTORA telescope observations (8 passes). All 8 publicly accessible objects are large tracked satellites — confirming the gap: the 1–10 cm target population is untracked and unarchived by design.  ·  TLE data: CelesTrak public GP endpoint, no registration. Auto-falls back to a 426-object physics-valid synthetic snapshot when the network is unreachable.  ·  Accuracy (synthetic held-out, n=400): size 83.8% · shape 95.2%. Real light curves are noisier — accuracy will degrade. Stated plainly in the app.
IBM AI Builders Challenge

Built with IBM Granite (ibm/granite-3-3-8b-instruct) via watsonx.ai. Granite is used in exactly three places — translating computed results into plain-language analyst briefs for each pipeline stage. It never produces or guesses numerical values. The app runs fully without credentials; Granite upgrades the prose, not the math.
